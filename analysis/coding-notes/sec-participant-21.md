## 3. Meanings and Practices

- Practice of mobility: a two-step process. Place an order on the website,
  then drive to the pantry to collect it.
- Meaning: for this client the "trip" includes the digital leg. She describes
  construction as "blocking the streets that access the website or the
  location" (Participant 21.docx, Interviewee 16:35:05), collapsing the online
  order and the physical pickup into one journey.

## 4. Misalignment

- Tension: the pantry's order-online-then-collect model works for a digitally
  fluent client but hides the wraparound services. She can place a food order
  but cannot find where financial help is described or how to start it.
  - Value at stake: equitable access to information.
  - "knowing what's available" is the first step she lacks. "It's not really described on the website very well." (Participant 21.docx, Interviewee 16:38:51 and 16:39:01)
  - concrete unanswered questions: "Do you need a separate account? Email address? Who you might be contacted by? If there's preferences on how to be contacted." (Participant 21.docx, Interviewee 16:41:31)
- Tension: road construction disrupts a pantry that people must physically
  reach on a schedule, with no fallback.
  - Value at stake: equitable access.
- Tension: the AI interview itself dropped out twice mid-conversation.
  - Value at stake: relational trust, and basic reliability of the
    automated instrument.
  - "Alex, are you still there?" (Participant 21.docx, Interviewee 16:36:38) and "Are you there, Alex?" (Participant 21.docx, Interviewee 16:41:31)
  - and it pushed to extend with "about a minute left" (Alex 16:39:27).

## Cross-bucket notes

- Best transcript in the SEC set for the digital layer. The website is now the
  front door: account, password, online order, saved link. This is a
  legibility and access system in its own right. It works smoothly for food
  and fails to surface everything else.
- The client's questions ("do you need a separate account, who contacts you,
  how do you want to be contacted") are a ready-made spec for what the
  services page should answer. Flag for design implications.
- "Blocking the streets that access the website or the location" is a
  telling slip: for this client the online and physical parts of the pantry
  are one continuous route, and a disruption to either is a disruption to the
  trip.
