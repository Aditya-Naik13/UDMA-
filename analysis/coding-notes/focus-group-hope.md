# Coding notes: Hope Food Pantry focus group (Insight Box, ideal meal)

- Source file: transcripts/Focus Groups/Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt
- Site: Hope Food Pantry (Martindale-Brightwood / Hillside area, Indianapolis)
- Type: Insight Box workshop, "recreate your ideal home meal" exercise around Thanksgiving, then facilitated discussion
- Language: English
- Format / locator scheme: txt, `HH:MM:SS Speaker N`. Speaker 2 is the lead researcher, Speaker 3 is the student facilitator. Speakers 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 are pantry clients. Two clients are named aloud in the transcript; names withheld here per anonymization, referred to by speaker number and role.
- Participants present: about 6 pantry clients, 1 lead researcher, 1 to 2 student facilitators
- Read in full: yes

## 1. Values

- Mutual care as an unquestioned duty: you show up for people who cannot get
  out, without interrogating them.
  - "Nothing. I don't ask questions. Look at you saying nothing. I don't get it. I don't ask questions." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:20:56, Speaker 5)
  - "I don't ask questions, I just show up, so, I guess I'm just a caregiver" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:40:10, Speaker 8)
  - "we're not just here for us, you know. Sometimes we give all of our stuff away" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:41:59, Speaker 8)
- The pantry as a last remaining safety net and an object of gratitude, tied
  to faith.
  - "The pantry is all we got to rely on." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:17:58, Speaker 1)
  - "I thank God for the pantry." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:49:16, Speaker 6)
  - "Financial support. I don't really care. I just bring it in with the Lord." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:15:04, Speaker 5)
- Dignity, pride, and stigma: asking for help carries shame, and some clients
  had to overcome their own earlier judgment of pantry users.
  - "it may be pride like i said it took me a minute because i used to get on her when i didn't live here i'm like what are you doing going there you don't need to go there but now i'm like oh yeah." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:48:56, Speaker 8)
  - "I ain't going to no pantry. I'll be the first one in line to do it. But that was me. I was like, no, that's for people who need it. I'm the person who need it now." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:22:35, Speaker 8)
  - "Some people are embarrassed by that because they have to ask for help." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:49:16, Speaker 6)
- Being known: clients value that pantry staff recognize them and that this
  recognition is what earns them small exceptions.
  - "she knows I come. So when she has to go to work on those Saturdays, she allows me to pick it up for her and my dad. And some people are like, nope, they got to be here." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:51:15, Speaker 8)
  - "we come here all the time. You see us all the time. You know us, who we are." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:51:13, Speaker 8)
- Self-reliance as an inherited value, now in tension with needing help.
  - "the way we were bought up, you can't take care. You know, you take care of yourself... can't nobody take care of you like you take care of yourself. So we weren't taught to come here and do stuff like that." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:22:47, Speaker 8)
- The ideal meal is defined by people and completeness, not food. Some clients
  wrote "nothing" on their plates and named emotional and financial needs
  instead.
  - "mashed potatoes it's filling they fulfill me completely chicken same thing, fill me up complete me everything else is nothing." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:09:15, Speaker 1)
  - "I want freedom from being broke." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:14:09, Speaker 1)
  - "My daddy's side to get closer... I need to meet them. I need to feel that space in." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:14:19, Speaker 1)
  - "we try to make sure for this particular holiday that everybody has enough to take home and, you know, nobody's hungry that day." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:10:46, Speaker 8)

## 2. Capabilities and Constraints

- Capability: sustained, organized informal care distribution. One client
  supplies a 93 year old homebound neighbor four days a week and also her
  father and her sister's household.
  - "I make sure she's got something at least four or five times a week. I can't go over there every day, but Monday through Thursday I'm at her house." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:20:31, Speaker 8)
  - "I've been doing this for 37 plus years." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:21:23, Speaker 5)
- Capability: deep, lifelong neighborhood knowledge. Clients can name who is
  homebound, which blocks, which stores overcharge.
  - "we've been in this neighborhood all our life... this is where we grew up." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:50:12, Speaker 8)
  - "I know a lot of people right on here on Hillside, they can't get out. They're older." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:20:13, Speaker 8)
- Capability: entrepreneurial skill and credentials. Clients list hair and
  nails work, making earrings and beads, a college degree, "one credit from my
  master", 18 years running a licensed in-home daycare.
  - "I make earrings and beads and stuff, too." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:24:28, Speaker 5)
  - "I had my own in-home daycare for 18 years... I'm licensed and everything, went to school and everything." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:36:15, Speaker 7)
- Capability: a car, which turns one person into a distribution node for
  several carless households.
  - "That's why I'm able to pick up stuff for other people who can't come, who don't have cars." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:42:51, Speaker 8)
- Constraint: income shocks and benefit cliffs. Hours cut from 40 to 20,
  daycare losing $20,000 a month, SNAP denied for being $74 over.
  - "I went from 40 hours to 20 hours" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:18:12, Speaker 8)
  - "my dad was $74 over. $74 over, so you don't get nothing... So it's like, okay, so he's going to starve because of $74." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:41:07, Speaker 8)
- Constraint / capability pair: a client wants to work in mental-health
  companionship and was on a training track, but a 2020 write-up after a
  domestic-violence incident blocks rehire.
  - "my goal was because community help you. They train you on moving up to your goals. I wanted to be a sitter. I like dealing with people with mental health issues... But I can't do that now. I got to start over, like completely over." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:31:36, Speaker 1)
  - "it's a hard time getting back when you got write-ups from 2020 and how their system is... they will pull up my old write-up... my application won't go through." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:31:04, Speaker 1)
- Constraint: no transportation and no license.
  - "I don't know how to drive. I've never had a license." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:47:10, Speaker 7)
  - "it's a lot of bus stops and a lot of times the bus stops to your destination is not close." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:41:41, Speaker 8)
- Constraint: food desert with predatory local pricing.
  - "if you look at the prices they're higher... significantly higher... because they know people who don't have a car don't have a choice" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:45:43, Speaker 8)
- Constraint: the pantry stocks food only, not the household and hygiene goods
  clients most struggle to afford.
  - "the household supplies is what I really come short on that I really can't afford." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:17:18, Speaker 5)
  - "if I could come to a pantry and get a pack of pads for her, that would save her $40 when she's only getting X amount of dollars a month." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:40:33, Speaker 8)

## 3. Meanings and Practices

- Meaning of mobility: getting to the pantry is inseparable from getting for
  others. Clients frame their own trips as a route serving multiple
  households.
  - "I come, actually, for some of my family members that can't get out... And it's a blessing, you know, to be able to, and I will go to several, you know, help them." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:43:57, Speaker 6)
  - "you're going to different places trying to just get enough to feed two or three households" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:20:13, Speaker 8)
- Practice: one client wakes at 6 a.m. to stand in line, then redistributes.
  - "You can go. Get your butt up at 6 o'clock like I did and go... No, this is going to Ms. [name]. Somebody who can't get out." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:49:41, Speaker 8) [third person's name replaced with [name]]
- Practice: pickup happens from inside cars. In winter the line is a line of
  vehicles, and people without a car are exposed.
  - "for the most part, this pantry, you're in the vehicle." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:48:11, Speaker 8)
  - "especially in the wintertime because it's cold. It's cold." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:47:50, Speaker 8)
- Practice: clients also move information. Much of the session is people
  briefing each other on a proposed homeless shelter, a data center, benefit
  changes, and job news.
  - "y'all need to let people know when y'all got issues like that so we can have choices" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:16:15, Speaker 5)
- Meaning: what is scarce is not just food but recognition of the care work
  already being done. The researcher names this back to them.
  - "instead of being afraid of the technology, I'm looking at the great asset in this community, carrying each other. That's very precious, kind of using the terms infrastructure." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:54:18, Speaker 2)

## 4. Misalignment

- Tension: the in-person pickup rule. To prevent fraud, most pantries require
  the beneficiary to appear in person, which directly excludes the homebound
  people clients are trying to feed.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need, and equitable access.
  - "We have a 98-year-old neighbor. She can't get out and get this. But then you can't take it to her because the person has to be here to get the benefits." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:19:42, Speaker 8)
  - "So how can you help somebody that can't get out to help themselves, but then they say they got to be here in person." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:19:56, Speaker 8)
  - the rule's rationale, in a client's words: "that's because people take advantage and mess it up for the people who really need to pick it up for other people." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:51:45, Speaker 8)
  - Hope is praised precisely because it bends this rule for known clients:
    "it's one of the only ones that allow you to get something for somebody that can't come." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:51:15, Speaker 8)
- Tension: zip code and service-area eligibility. Pantries with more goods are
  gated by where you live.
  - Value at stake: equitable access.
  - "That's out east. You have to live in their area codes." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:43:35, Speaker 7)
  - "You can't go under the zip code." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:43:39, Speaker 8)
- Tension: pantry hours. Distribution is every other Saturday morning, which
  is exactly when many clients and caregivers work.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need.
  - "she has meetings sometimes on Saturdays, and then she can't come." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:41:39, Speaker 8)
  - "I come to this one for the most part because it's every other Saturday." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:41:34, Speaker 8)
- Tension: standardized food-only stock vs actual need for hygiene and
  household goods. Efficient to source and distribute one category, but it
  misses what clients cannot buy.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need.
  - "They basically just have food... They don't have household goods. Things like that are expensive." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:41:22, Speaker 8)
- Tension: hiring systems that surface old records and block rehire despite
  "rehireable" status.
  - Value at stake: equitable access to work, named by the paper's broader
    argument about efficiency-driven systems.
  - "they will look up your history, even though I'm rehireable, but they will pull up my old write-up... And my application won't go through." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:31:04, Speaker 1)
- Tension named by the researcher: institutions dropping "community" from
  their language as they pivot to workforce and efficiency framing.
  - Value at stake: community participation.
  - "there's an office called like community engagement now it is called workforce development and community engagement so you took the community out" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:57:03, Speaker 2)
- Tension: automation arriving in low-wage service work without consultation.
  - Value at stake: equitable access, community participation.
  - "White Castle now white castle has an automated things not even nobody taking your order you walk up and she's like hi I'm Julia your assistant please say your order when you're ready." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:26:48, Speaker 8)

## 5. Provocation

- Speculative prompt present: yes. The lead researcher raises driverless
  vehicles twice.
  - "have you heard about the driverless vehicle... those are things like all the automations actually replacing our labor force right" (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:26:08, Speaker 2)
  - "if there's a car which doesn't need any human drivers and can run 24 hours seven days and then somebody can donate it to your community. What do you think? You know, what could be the usages of it." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:46:44, Speaker 2)
- Participant reactions: cautiously positive, framed immediately through
  personal mobility gaps, not logistics.
  - "I think I would take a car. You know why?... Because I don't know how to drive. I've never had a license. If a car could drive me when I say put the address in, like the Uber, I could put the address in and I'll just sit there and go, la, listen to my music, dance, and the car just be rolling on where I'm going." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:47:10, Speaker 7)
  - "I think it would be used because it's a lot of bus stops and a lot of times the bus stops to your destination is not close." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:47:41, Speaker 8)
  - "So I believe that they would utilize it, especially in the wintertime because it's cold." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:47:56, Speaker 8)
- Conditions and concerns attached: raised against a backdrop of deep worry
  that automation is already taking their jobs (White Castle kiosks, jobs
  reclassified as non-professional). The AV is welcomed as a rider service but
  the same participants are alarmed about automation of labor generally.
  - "it's not fair for all of us to be dominated, replaced by all this kind of technology conversation." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:53:35, Speaker 2, researcher framing that participants agreed with: "Oh, yeah, it's horrible.")
- Who they thought it would help: non-drivers, people whose bus stop is far
  from their destination, people exposed to winter weather in the pantry line.
- Adjacent, not AV: bus vouchers are volunteered as the low-tech version of
  the same need. "bus vouchers would be good for people, too." (Plaud Hope_Food_Pantry-transcript.txt, 00:48:32, Speaker 8)

## Cross-bucket notes

- The strongest single image for the paper: a line of cars at a Saturday
  pantry, one driver picking up for three households and a 93 year old
  neighbor, while the rule says the neighbor has to come in person. The
  community has already built the distribution network the rule forbids.
- The AV question landed in a room already frightened about automation. Any
  design recommendation has to hold both: people want the ride, and they do
  not want to be "replaced."
- "you deserve to be paid... what you guys are doing with a good heart should
  be recognized" (00:54:18, Speaker 2). The researcher explicitly frames the
  care mobility as uncompensated labor and economic infrastructure.
- Multiple community-planning grievances (homeless shelter, data center,
  property tax) share a structure with the AV question: things are "put in our
  neighborhood" and "the people that got the money, they're going to do it
  anyhow. It don't matter if you protest or not." (00:45:02, Speaker 7)

## Coder uncertainty

- Speaker attribution in the txt is imperfect. Long turns sometimes contain
  crosstalk from more than one person (e.g. 00:15:04, 00:33:09). Quotes are
  attributed to the labeled speaker but some lines within a turn may be
  another person.
- Auto-transcription noise is heavy in places ("four peas from life" at
  00:08:43 likely mis-hears something else; "pens" and "insurers" recur and
  appear to mean incontinence products / Depends). Quotes chosen avoid the
  worst-garbled passages.
- Two participants are named aloud (00:37:45, 00:43:57); names deliberately
  omitted here.
- The federal job-reclassification claims (00:30:09, 00:27:39) are
  participants' accounts, quoted as their statements, not verified.
