# MaSS New Codes

## JUST_TRUST

**Definition:** Trust that people place in those who provide their rides, food, or services, built through an ongoing relationship: being known, treated with respect, understood, and able to count on providers to keep showing up.

| Subcode | Definition |
|---|---|
| BEING_KNOWN | People rely on help from those who know and recognise them, such as familiar staff, volunteers, neighbours, or family, rather than strangers or formal systems. |
| RESPECTFUL_TREATMENT | How staff, drivers, or volunteers treat a person while they use the service, whether with kindness and respect or with rudeness and dismissal, shapes whether they keep coming. |
| LANGUAGE_RELATIONSHIP | Being able to communicate with providers in one's own language shapes whether help feels trustworthy and usable. |
| FOLLOW_THROUGH | Providers keep their commitments: services continue, promises are met, and people are not dropped without warning. |

## JUST_FLEXIBILITY

**Definition:** Whether mobility and services can adapt to needs that differ between people and change over time: when people can travel, how often and how much help they need, and how trips fit around the rest of their lives.

## MISALIGNMENT

**Definition:** A gap between what an efficiency-driven system is built to optimise and what the community needs or values. The gap may be stated outright or implied, and expressed calmly or in reaction.

| Subcode | Definition |
|---|---|
| IDEAL_VS_ACTUAL_TRAVELER | A system is designed around an imagined typical traveler whose circumstances differ from those of the people who actually rely on it. |

## PURPOSE_EXCLUSION

**Definition:** Questioning what a technology, service, or piece of infrastructure is for, and who decided that purpose, rather than how it is designed or run. This includes seeing the same pattern in other projects that have arrived in the community.

| Subcode | Definition |
|---|---|
| PATTERN_OF_IMPOSITION | The community describes other technologies, facilities, or projects being placed in their neighbourhood without them, showing the arrival of an AV as part of a wider pattern. |

## INPUT_PART_ABSENT

**Definition:** Community members have no part in deciding how the mobility or food services they rely on are planned, designed, run, or changed.

| Subcode | Definition |
|---|---|
| CONSULT_NO_POWER | Community members are asked for their views but have no power to make sure those views shape the decision, which stays with whoever ran the process. |
| INCLUSIVE_W/O_DECISION_POWER | Community members are visibly included, given a seat, a role, or public recognition, but that inclusion gives them no real power over decisions, so inclusion is performed rather than practised. |
| DEMOTIVATION_Ill_TREATMENT | A person holds back from speaking up or taking part because past experiences of being ignored, dismissed, or treated badly have taught them their input will not matter. |
| EXPERTS_CENTERED | Professionals, officials, or researchers are treated as the ones who define the problem and decide the solution, while residents whose mobility is affected are treated as data or subjects rather than as people with knowledge. |

## INPUT_PART_PRESENT

**Definition:** Community members have a real part in deciding how the mobility or food services they rely on are planned, designed, run, or changed, and their input shapes the outcome.

| Subcode | Definition |
|---|---|
| COMMUNITY_BUILT | Residents or community organisations create their own plan, service, or solution instead of only responding to one made by others. |
| COGOVERN | Community members share binding authority with an agency or provider over how a service is designed or run, acting as co-decision-makers rather than advisers. |
