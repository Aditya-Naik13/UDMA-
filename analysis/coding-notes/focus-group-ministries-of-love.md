# Coding notes: Ministries of Love focus group (Insight Box, ideal meal)

- Source file: transcripts/Focus Groups/Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt
- Site: Ministries of Love pantry (Indianapolis, near Fountain Square / Beech Grove references)
- Type: Insight Box workshop, "recreate your ideal meal" exercise, then facilitated discussion
- Language: English
- Format / locator scheme: txt, `HH:MM:SS Speaker N`. Speaker 1 is the lead researcher, Speaker 2 is the student facilitator. Speakers 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 are pantry clients, most of them an extended family sharing one multi-unit house. Several first names are spoken in the transcript; withheld here, referred to by speaker number and role.
- Participants present: about 5 pantry clients (several related), 1 lead researcher, 1 student facilitator
- Read in full: yes

## 1. Values

- Mutual aid extended past the family to homeless neighbors who come to the
  door. Redistribution is routine, not exceptional.
  - "it ain't just for me and my family it's other people in our neighborhood that know there's homeless and there i got different ones i'll come up to the door and you got something i can eat." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:46:25, Speaker 3)
  - "i make extra, like, meals, you know, somebody comes by every day and somebody's getting something." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:46:46, Speaker 3)
  - "Since a lot of the pensions give a lot of the same stuff, we give it to other people." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:46:42, Speaker 5) [transcription likely "pantries" not "pensions"]
- Intergenerational family living as a deliberate model of care.
  - "Yeah, we're like the old school where you got all the family living. That's a blessing. We're in one house, but in different units, so we got our space." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:26:11, Speaker 3)
  - "normally, you know, you lose your husband, you would be left alone, but I got all everybody. That's just stuck with us." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:35:25, Speaker 5)
- Being listened to is itself a service, distinct from getting resources.
  - "Being dealing with suicide, mental issues, health issues, or food issues. Sometimes you just need someone to listen to you." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:42:32, Speaker 3)
- Peer help between clients: one participant who works in real estate offers to
  help another fix a property-tax misclassification.
  - "I work in real estate on the side. I can try and help you navigate that." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:09:10, Speaker 7)
- Difficulty asking for help, again.
  - "No, I'm not good at asking." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:24:04, Speaker 1) [note: this line is attributed to Speaker 1 in the transcript but reads as a client's words reported or echoed; see coder uncertainty]
- The ideal meal is a lost person. Several plates are memorials.
  - "I lost my husband, so everything is like a memory to me." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:07:02, Speaker 5)
  - "it's one of the last meals I had with him that I remember, because the last year of his life, he didn't get to eat. He was on a feeding machine." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:33:44, Speaker 5)

## 2. Capabilities and Constraints

- Capability: knowledge of the resource landscape. Participants coach each
  other on produce days, Community Compass, 211, cheap eyeglasses, and other
  pantries.
  - "if you go to the community cupboards online... It's like 2-1-1. They give you help with loading gantries, food gantries... Community cupboards does a lot and they can refer you to a lot of places" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:23:13, Speaker 3)
  - "have you heard about Southeastern Community Center?... They have a produce day every Wednesday." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:21:43, Speaker 1, confirmed by clients)
- Capability: self-taught cooking and adaptation for health needs. One
  participant taught herself to cook over five years at home with a disabling
  sciatic injury, and is now converting the household's meals for a diabetic
  spouse.
  - "I'm a self-taught cook, I taught myself how to cook, because my mom didn't really teach me... I had hip issues about 10 years ago and so i was at home for five years because my sciatic nerve went out... so i taught myself how to cook since i was home all day" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:30:13, Speaker 7)
  - "I have switched almost all our meals... I have switched the hamburger to turkey instead." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:16:24, Speaker 7)
- Capability: shared-household logistics. The family runs a rotating meal
  system across units.
  - "Everybody goes to somebody else's different days." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:35:09, Speaker 7)
  - "We're doing it at least five days a week." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:35:18, Speaker 3)
- Capability/constraint pair: a participant has a job orientation lined up but
  a broken-down car may cost her the opportunity.
  - "I need to talk to him and see if he can work on my car, so if he can't work on my car, I ain't gonna make it to that interview." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:51:32, Speaker 5)
  - "I'm trying to go back to work, and then my car is broken down" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:10:01, Speaker 5)
- Constraint: benefit and cost pressures cascade. A widow cannot afford
  glasses she needs because property taxes rose after her husband's death.
  - "I need glasses and I ain't been able to afford to get them yet... I'm trying to pay property taxes and everything since I lost my husband is all on me." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:08:12, Speaker 5)
- Constraint: a bureaucratic misclassification. Her house is taxed as
  commercial, and she never received the homestead deduction in eight years.
  - "they got me commercialized instead of home, so I'm getting charged for commercial property... So that made the difference in my deal being $4,000 or $2,000." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:09:03, Speaker 5)
  - "I just found out that I've never gotten homestead in none of the years... And I've lived there eight years." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:09:41, Speaker 5)
- Constraint: medication interactions the health system has not resolved. A
  diabetic participant's heart and psychiatric medications spike his blood
  sugar.
  - "two of his medications, Spike his blood sugar up, so what's the sense in being on blood sugar medicine if your heart medication... Spike your sugar." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:17:38, Speaker 7)
- Constraint: transportation, named as the reason neighbors cannot reach the
  pantry.
  - "Transportation's a big issue. Yeah, it's a big thing for a lot of people." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:47:01, Speaker 3)

## 3. Meanings and Practices

- Practice: the pantry visit is one node in a redistribution circuit. Surplus
  and duplicate items are cooked into extra meals and handed out daily to
  people who come to the door.
  - "weekend it ain't just for me and my family... somebody comes by every day and somebody's getting something." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:46:25 to 00:46:46, Speaker 3)
- Practice: mobility inside the household. Meals rotate between units by who is
  short on what.
  - "Well, someone's short on something or somebody ain't got something" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:35:12, Speaker 5)
- Meaning: transportation is the difference between reaching a job and not.
  For this group the pressing trip is to work and to a job orientation, not
  only to the pantry.
- Practice: information mobility. Much of the session is clients passing each
  other referrals, phone numbers, and warnings (which pantry stopped stocking
  meat, which store is cheaper, how to get homestead fixed).
- Meaning named by a client: outreach should be physical and face to face
  because not everyone is reachable by phone.
  - "it's always a good resource because not everybody has the phone. sometimes just putting a couple people out handing out wires." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:45:16, Speaker 3) [likely "flyers" not "wires"]

## 4. Misalignment

- Tension: a nearby pantry replaced first-come-first-served with a
  card-shuffler lottery. Intended to be fairer or more orderly, it destroys
  predictability and can cost a person an entire afternoon.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need, and respect for people's
    time.
  - "it ain't even like first come first serve anymore. It's they've put it like in a card shuffler the way they do it. And so you can be sitting there at two o'clock. They don't even open in the doors until like three thirty and they don't even start until four. So you can be the first ones in line and not get out. Until six o'clock at night." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:22:13, Speaker 7)
- Tension: no choice of food. Standardized boxes are efficient to pack but
  leave diabetic and health-restricted clients with starch they should not
  eat.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need.
  - "you cannot choose food items like we choose but it's not... it's not healthy like what they're supposed to be most of it's like starchy" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:21:02, Speaker 7)
  - workaround through relationship: "my husband volunteers and a lot of times he's the one handing out the meat, so he kind of, yeah." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:21:32, Speaker 7)
- Tension: a pantry narrowing its stock to snacks and dropping meat and
  produce.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need.
  - "they used to give out meats and stuff, but they stopped giving out meats. They rarely have any like anything. It's pretty much nothing but snacks." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:22:13, Speaker 7)
- Tension: resource navigation gated behind a smartphone and the internet.
  - Value at stake: equitable access.
  - "if you go to the community cupboards online, on your phone, a lot of people have smartphones." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:23:13, Speaker 3), answered later by "not everybody has the phone." (00:45:16, Speaker 3)
- Tension: a mandatory monthly nutrition class where the program failed to
  tell enrolled participants it had started, so they missed the first two.
  - Value at stake: relational trust, and flexibility for variable need.
  - "we've missed the first two classes because they didn't let us know that the classes had even started" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:29:25, Speaker 7)
- Tension: property-tax system defaulted a widow's home to a commercial
  classification and never applied the homestead deduction, doubling her bill.
  - Value at stake: equitable access, and the broader argument that
    efficiency and legibility systems put their error cost onto the resident.
  - "So that made the difference in my deal being $4,000 or $2,000." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:09:10, Speaker 5)
- Tension: public transit that the intended riders cannot afford.
  - Value at stake: equitable access.
  - "what's the sense in having a public transportation if you still can't afford the public transportation" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:49:50, Speaker 7)

## 5. Provocation

- Speculative prompt present: yes. The facilitator raises AVs directly.
  - "there's these AV autonomous vehicles that are coming out, if there was a vehicle that was available 24-7 that can take people places, do you think that would benefit the community if they were able to use these vehicles." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:48:17, Speaker 2)
- Participant reaction: qualified yes, with an immediate safety and misuse
  concern rooted in neighborhood knowledge.
  - "I think it would, but it would have to be on issues, you know. what reason are they going to the grocery store or they just run into someone's house because you got to be careful with that right okay they could be using it for drugs" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:48:34, Speaker 3)
  - "when you get into the neighborhoods you know we are very aware where we live so trying... so we're in the hood so if we make a vehicle accessible it may not just be used for needs but for other things" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:48:34 to 00:49:07, Speaker 3 and Speaker 2)
- Concrete design condition offered by participants: restrict destinations.
  - "you need to put in a destination see where the destination goes yeah limit the destinations to like pantries and grocery store" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:49:07 to 00:49:19, Speaker 3 and Speaker 7)
- Who they thought it would help: older people who cannot drive and cannot
  afford the bus on a fixed income.
  - "like older even older people they can't drive and stuff... with them being on fixed income they can't afford the bus all the time" (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:49:19, Speaker 7)
- Adjacent, not AV: bus passes are again volunteered as the immediate fix.
  - "it would be good to pass out some bus passes sometimes, you know, to different people." (Plaud Ministries of Love transcript.txt, 00:48:11, Speaker 3)

## Cross-bucket notes

- The AV response here is the most design-actionable in the corpus: yes, but
  with destination limits, because an open-ended vehicle in this neighborhood
  will be used for things other than errands. This is a community member
  proposing a specific control, not rejecting the technology.
- The card-shuffler pantry and the missed nutrition classes are two clean
  examples of process changes made without the people affected, both
  experienced as a loss of control over time.
- The memorial plates (a meatloaf meal, a husband on a feeding machine, blue
  and purple markers) show that for grieving participants the "ideal meal"
  prompt surfaced loss, not aspiration. Handle with care in the synthesis.
- A participant disclosed a suicide attempt and ongoing vision loss
  (00:38:24 onward). Do not quote the disclosure itself in the synthesis; the
  codable point is that her spouse asked her to stop working while she
  recovers, which is a household adapting to variable capacity.

## Coder uncertainty

- Speaker attribution is unreliable. Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 are researchers,
  but several lines tagged to them read as client speech or crosstalk (e.g.
  00:24:04, 00:49:07). Client quotes are attributed to the tagged client
  speaker; treat the researcher-tagged lines with caution.
- Auto-transcription errors are frequent: "pensions" for pantries, "gantries"
  for pantries, "wires" for flyers, "David shot" for a diabetes injection
  brand. Quotes selected try to avoid the worst of these; bracketed notes flag
  the likely intended word.
- Several first names are spoken (Michelle, Gina, Joyce, Connie, and others);
  all omitted here.
- Medication and tax-dollar figures are participants' own accounts, quoted as
  their statements, not verified.
