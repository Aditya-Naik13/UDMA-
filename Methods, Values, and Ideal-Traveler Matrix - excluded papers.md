# Methods, Values, and Ideal-Traveler Matrix: excluded tracker papers

The matrix draws on the 39-paper literature-review tracker. 34 of those
papers have a matrix row. The 6 below were assessed (abstract, method,
conclusion, and a skim of the body) and left out, with the reason recorded
here so the decision is not silently lost. If the paper's section of the
paper later needs one of them, the note says what it would contribute.

## Excluded

### Public transport inclusion and active aging: A systematic review on elderly mobility (Zhang & Yang, 2024)
Bibliometric knowledge-mapping plus qualitative review of 138 elderly-mobility
papers; proposes a research framework for public-transport inclusion. No
participatory method, no design authority over a service, no decision-maker
versus lived-experience content. It is a research-agenda review. Uses the
capability approach. Nothing the matrix's frame can hold onto.

### Assessing equity in infrastructure investment distribution among U.S. cities (Chen et al., 2025)
Quantitative study: Social Vulnerability Index plus municipal infrastructure
expenditure data, deep-learning statistical analysis, estimates of the
Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act's equity implications. The unit of
analysis is fiscal allocation among cities, which is policy design, explicitly
out of scope for the "who holds design authority / what is being designed"
columns. Usable only as a one-line citation for the efficiency frame:
"conventional approaches to infrastructure planning and investment
decision-making have predominantly focused on maximizing economic efficiency
and investment returns, often overlooking the broader impact on diverse
regions and social groups" (abstract).

### Quantification of latent travel demand across age groups with a focus on older adults: a theoretical framework with empirical evidence from China (Yin & Xi, 2026)
Quantitative survey study: 664 samples from Ningbo, age-stratified regression
on a four-dimensional latent-demand framework (health barriers, functional
capacity, social participation, technology acceptance). No participation, no
fieldwork engagement with lived experience, no design-authority content,
non-US. One finding echoes other rows (Wander et al. on Universal Basic
Mobility): "age-friendly policy should move beyond physical infrastructure
improvement alone and place greater emphasis on promoting social
participation." Not enough on its own for a row.

### Beyond research delays / Investigating unfulfilled travel needs (NHTS 2017) (Zhang et al., 2026)
Quantitative secondary-data model: a logit model with interaction effects on
2017 NHTS data, identifying determinants of "reduce day-to-day travel" among
people with travel-limiting conditions, with an intersectional (gender, race,
poverty) lens. No participatory method, no design authority, no
decision-maker-versus-lived content. Corroborates suppressed and unmet travel
demand among disabled subgroups (notably a Hispanic disabled population), a
point already carried by the disability-justice fieldwork rows.

### Urban planning, the built environment, and subjective well-being: A review of recent research advances (Mouratidis, 2026)
Broad narrative literature review of built-environment and subjective-well-being
research since 2021, organised around a pathways framework (walkability,
proximity, green space, housing, 15-minute city, urban AI). Mobility and
accessibility are one of five categories. No participatory-method,
design-authority, or ideal-traveler content relevant to the matrix. Too far
from the frame.

### Who faces travel constraints? Analyzing household and individual travel challenges in automobile-dependent central Texas (Situ, 2025)
Quantitative study: a household Housing and Transportation Cost-to-Income Ratio
plus an individual Intra-personal Travel Constraints propensity model, run on
NextGen NHTS data for Greater Austin, classifying household constraint
profiles. No participation, no fieldwork, no design content. Distinctive
points, kept here in case they are useful: the constraint-versus-choice
framing ("what may appear as a limitation in one context could be a deliberate
choice in another"; "planners generally view high travel costs and limited
infrastructure as constraints, [but] these factors are not always
burdensome"), and the finding that single-mother households, primarily Black
and Hispanic/Latino, carry compounded intra-personal travel constraints.

## Borderline calls, included as thin rows

- **Beyond research delays: science funding disruptions limit community
  participation in climate and infrastructure planning** (Contreras et al.,
  2026): an essay, not a study, but it states the techno-rational planning
  frame precisely and gives a one-sentence technical-versus-lived example
  (a lit-street walking route). Row added.
- **Implementation science frameworks for active and sustainable
  transportation interventions with a focus on equity: A scoping review**
  (McCullogh et al., 2026): names the "pathway of actors responsible for
  funding, design, planning, and construction" and finds 17 of 26 studies
  ignored equity and that participation is treated as a time-and-budget cost.
  Row added.
- **Mobility Injustice by Design** (Jensen, 2026): only a publisher preview
  (front matter, preface, first pages of Chapter 1) was available, but that
  much supplies the "mobility is made and therefore contestable" thesis and
  the pedestrian "green period" signal-timing example. Row added, heavily
  caveated.
