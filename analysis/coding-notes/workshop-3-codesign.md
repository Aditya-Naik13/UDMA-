## 1. Values

- Stakeholders explicitly frame social service as relational, not
  transactional, and name this as something AV design would have to be
  deliberate about.
  - "Social service meaning relational engagement not transactional" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: Social service meaning relational engagement not transactional, cluster header)
  - "AV barrier: it needs to be intentional about the relation" (same cluster)
  - "Maintaining the human relation" (same cluster)
- A transit-equity value: serve where people actually need to go, and treat
  accessibility as a mission even when the economics resist it.
  - "IndyGo's intent is to serve most people with access, where they need to go, not everyone. IndyGo serving everyone, not feasible. IndyGo foundation perspective: yes wants to make transport accessible and equitable" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Fare)
- A value that the end user's burden should be the measure of success, against
  standard financial return.
  - "Measuring impact: the discussion questions traditional financial ROI for public good projects, suggesting a need for new metrics that measure the weights of burden and capture the ultimate benefit to the end user" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: ROI)

## 2. Capabilities and Constraints

- Constraint: transportation is a top barrier for pantry clients but is
  chronically underfunded and never funded as a primary service.
  - "Transportation funding gap: a poll of non profits revealed that while transportation is a top client barrier it receives disproportionately low and restricted funding. It is often bundled as a need with grants for other areas rather than being funded as a primary service" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Funding & decision criteria)
  - "A survey among 136 organizations, with funding up to 340 million: 8 different basic needs, only 1.2M was for funding. Main issue in transportation but no funding" (same cluster)
- Constraint: fixed-route public transit cannot serve individuals or the first
  and last mile to a pantry.
  - "Indy Go AV can't serve singular person." (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: limitations of macro transit)
  - "Microtransit: busstop 1 mile away from pantry. AV can drop off user to the pantry to fill the walking gap" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Micro transit)
- Constraint: pantry hours collide with work hours.
  - "Time is a factor - beyond 9-5. Food pantry works on Saturday mornings - many families work at that time" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: Time (End Users))
  - "Working jobs, miss out pantry" (same cluster)
- Constraint: rules set by federal food programs, not clients, create the
  friction.
  - "Challenges for shepherd is not people but how the system is set (federal program creates challenges)" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: limitations of macro transit)
  - "TFAB (govt program with lots of reporting)" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Shepherds)
- Constraint: homebound people are excluded even from the conversation about
  transportation solutions.
  - "Homebound people miss out on transportation ideations" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: Home Bound)
- Capability framing is thin here. The nearest asset named is that MPO has a
  standing committee that includes transit and micro-transit riders
  representing the community.
  - "MPO has a Serve committee which has users of both transit users and micro transit users representing the community" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Micro transit)

## 3. Meanings and Practices

- Meaning: for homebound people, the delivery is not a drop-off, it is their
  human contact for the day.
  - "Delivery person might be the only point of human contact the home bound people might have" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: Home Bound)
  - "Indygo: home delivery might be the only personal interaction" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: Social service meaning relational engagement not transactional)
- Practice: the existing system already moves people to food and food to
  people through several overlapping programs (Groceries to Go, TFAB, DAB) run
  by Gleaners through Shepherd, with heavy coordination cost.
  - "Communication is complex, so many people involved" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Communication friction among stakeholder)
- Practice / meaning debated: whether the AV should move food, move people, or
  move people with food.
  - "Targeted use case with beta volunteers: moving people with food, or move people to food" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Emergency Box, Shepherd/Gleaners)
  - "Shepherd: AV - more useful to bringing people. More useful to design it so it can be served right out of pantry" (same)
- Route design as practice: routes are drawn by housing density, schools, and
  infrastructure counts, and food deserts are named as a factor but not yet
  actually used.
  - "MPO topic: food desert is a decision factor for route. Monitoring food desert, but not yet" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Route design)

## 4. Misalignment

- Tension: planning decisions are made for neighbors without them, and their
  needs are not accommodated. Stakeholders name this directly.
  - Value at stake: community participation, and equitable access.
  - "Neighbor have ideas imposed on them. Their needs are not accommodated" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: People centered, Input/decision making)
  - "Lack of experience in planning and the perspective for planning" (same)
- Tension: transit is optimized to serve the most people efficiently, which by
  design does not serve the singular, the homebound, or the off-route
  household.
  - Value at stake: equitable access, and flexibility for variable need.
  - "IndyGo's intent is to serve most people with access, where they need to go, not everyone." (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Fare)
  - "Indy Go AV can't serve singular person." (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: limitations of macro transit)
- Tension: funders bundle transportation under other grant categories and fund
  it minimally, so the top client barrier stays unaddressed as a service in
  its own right.
  - Value at stake: equitable access.
  - "It is often bundled as a need with grants for other areas rather than being funded as a primary service" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Funding & decision criteria)
- Tension: standard financial ROI is the wrong yardstick for a public-good
  mobility service, but it is still the yardstick projects get judged by.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need, and the social-service
    framing itself.
  - "the discussion questions traditional financial ROI for public good projects, suggesting a need for new metrics that measure the weights of burden" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: ROI)
- Tension: automaker interest in AV adoption is openly named as a driver of
  infrastructure investment, which points the design toward adoption rate as
  the success metric rather than community benefit.
  - Value at stake: Mobility as Social Service versus product-adoption logic.
  - "Biggest ROI of AV as a social service is the adoption rate of AV" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Current Infrastructure)
  - board annotation: "More AV adoption, Serving Automakers interest in building infrastructure" (transcript_workshop3.txt, CROSS-CLUSTER CONNECTIONS, item 5)
- Tension: federal food-program rules and reporting, not clients, generate the
  friction Shepherd deals with.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need.
  - "Challenges for shepherd is not people but how the system is set (federal program creates challenges)" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: limitations of macro transit)

## 5. Provocation

- Speculative prompt present: yes. Board framing questions are "Barriers for
  AVs adoption", "How would AV works in terms of service?", "What would AV
  Address".
- Range of reactions:
  - Skepticism that AV is a general solution. "AV cannot be designed for everyone. It's not the next big solution." (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: People centered, Personalized services); "All personas are not the same. They have different needs" (same).
  - Regional and institutional reluctance to adopt AVs at all. "Within MPO, reluctancy & hesitancy within midwest about AVs. Also lack of knowledge + understanding about the technology in general" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: Barriers for AVs adoption, cluster: Awareness).
  - Most concrete positive use case: small AVs as micro-transit shuttles
    closing the first and last mile between transit lines and pantries. "using small AV's as micro transit shuttles to solve the first and last mile problem, connecting locations like food pantries to main public transit lines" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Micro transit).
  - AV as capacity multiplier for pantries and the network. "IHN: AV as a tool to increase capacity" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: How would AV works in terms of service?, cluster: Current Infrastructure).
  - A cited pilot statistic: "G2G Program: 85% success with AV, 15% face issues, but ROI" (transcript_workshop3.txt, SECTION: What would AV address, cluster: Features of AV's). Coder note: source and definition of "success" not given in transcript.
- Conditions and concerns attached: the AV must be intentionally relational
  and not replace the human contact of delivery; it cannot be one-size-fits-
  all; funding likely has to come through federal grants routed via non
  profits, modeled on how IHN already grants refrigerators; weather, traffic,
  and construction threaten predictability.
- Who stakeholders thought it would help or exclude: helps homebound
  neighbors, off-route households, and the first-and-last-mile walker; risk of
  excluding anyone if it is designed "for everyone" rather than for specific
  personas, and homebound people are already left out of the ideation.

## Cross-bucket notes

- "Delivery person might be the only point of human contact the home bound
  people might have" is the single most important line in this transcript for
  the paper. It is the relational-trust argument stated by a planner, and it
  directly complicates any efficiency case for removing the human from
  delivery.
- The board explicitly connects "Social service meaning relational engagement
  not transactional" to "Input/decision making" with an arrow, i.e. the
  stakeholders themselves link being relational to giving neighbors a say.
- "Arthur" recurs as the homebound-neighbor persona standing in for the hardest
  case. Useful as a design device, but not a person.
