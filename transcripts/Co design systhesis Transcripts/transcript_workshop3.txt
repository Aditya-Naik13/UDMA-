SECTION: BARRIERS FOR AVS ADOPTION
================================================================================

## Funding & decision criteria
- School bus has grants - is federal. Indy Go: from federal state, budget approved by city council
- IndyGo: Food Insecurity supported, at 31% food insecurity, 29% transport, childcare, education
- Transportation funding gap: a poll of non profits revealed that while transportation is a top client barrier it receives disproportionately low and restricted funding. It is often bundled as a need with grants for other areas rather than being funded as a primary service
- Challenges IHN: funding, FD network support, duplication, grants duplication
- Public safety, BRT System, public mass transit fund, 11% of funding to Indy Go for bus replacement
- Indy Go has higher fares x Social Service Agents. A survey among 136 organizations, with funding up to 340 million: 8 different basic needs, only 1.2M was for funding. Main issue in transportation but no funding

## Awareness
- Within MPO, reluctancy & hesitancy within midwest about AVs. Also lack of knowledge + understanding about the technology in general
- MPO: reluctancy is adopting AV
- Conception of AV → reluctance of AV adoption → lack knowledge → barrier that fund only projects this is brought to us

## Fare
- IndyGo: serve the most people in access. Foundation is different
- IndyGo increases fares → bus passes are given out to mitigate that (not a problem)
- IndyGo's intent is to serve most people with access, where they need to go, not everyone. IndyGo serving everyone → not feasible. IndyGo foundation perspective: yes wants to make transport accessible and equitable

## Route design
- Density of houses → routes have been made intentionally
- Monitoring food desert → MPO, whenever there is a project
- MPO topic: food desert is a decision factor for route. Monitoring food desert, but not yet
- MPO route depends on numbers of houses, schools, and other essential infrastructure
- Transportation is made faster and quicker → route design

## IHN
- IHN redundancy with Gleaners (funding grants)
- IHN - collaborative support
- IHN support food pantry network (community compass)

## Shepherds
- Gleaners operates 3 programs through shepherds:
  1. Groceries to go
  2. TFAB (govt program with lots of reporting)
  3. DAB (food pickup across)

## Micro transit
- Microtransit: busstop 1 mile away from pantry. AV can drop off user to the pantry to fill the walking gap
- Microtransit: a key idea is using small AV's as micro transit shuttles to solve the first and last mile problem, connecting locations like food pantries to main public transit lines
- Indy Go: Indygo foundation helps connect Indygo. Indygo services are not always close enough to Arthur to go to food pantry
- MPO has a Serve committee which has users of both transit users and micro transit users representing the community
- AV implementation idea by MPO: pantry opens at a particular time, we can do inter-shuttling with bigger shuttles, substitute BRT line
