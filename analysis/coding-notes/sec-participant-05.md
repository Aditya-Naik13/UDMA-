## 4. Misalignment

- Tension: the pantry's wraparound programs are delivered as in-person group
  settings, which is a barrier for a socially anxious client. The fix she
  names is not more contact but more predictability.
  - Value at stake: flexibility for variable need (the program assumes a
    comfort with group participation that not all clients have).
  - "What makes you feel unsure... about programs like job coaching?" / "Being around others that I'm not familiar with." (Participant 05.docx, Alex 15:58:24, Interviewee 15:58:30)
- Tension: program information (times, dates) is not communicated clearly
  enough for her to plan around her anxiety.
  - Value at stake: equitable access.
  - "Having the right information with the right times and dates." (Participant 05.docx, Interviewee 15:57:03)
- Contrary evidence (positive): the drive-through model, criticized elsewhere
  in the corpus as impersonal, is here a genuine accessibility feature. The
  same design choice serves this client and underserves the client who wants
  to be seen and greeted.

## Cross-bucket notes

- This transcript is the counterweight to the "drive-through is impersonal"
  reading. For an anxious client, low-contact and predictable is the point. A
  design implication: the pantry likely needs both a high-contact and a
  low-contact path, chosen by the client.
- "Knowing that I have a community to help" alongside "being able to just
  drive through" is not a contradiction to smooth over; it is the finding.
  Community can mean a safety net you do not have to socially perform for.
