- Type: AI-agent interview (interviewer "Alex"), in Spanish; transcript is the English translation
- Language: Spanish
- Format / locator scheme: docx, `Name • HH:MM:SS`. Quotes are translated, not verbatim.
- Participants present: 1 client (single mother, about two years at the pantry), 1 AI interviewer
- Read in full: yes

## 1. Values

- Being listened to and given confidence, and staying in contact.
  - "here with them, they give you, they give you confidence, they listen to you... anything, they let me know through a message or text message or WhatsApp message." (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:27:26; translated)
- Warmth of reception as the thing that makes help usable.
  - "the people who are going to receive me have a nice attitude like those here at this pantry" (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:29:04; translated)
- Motivation: her children and the purpose of having migrated.
  - "Continuing the purpose of coming to this country, my children primarily, and continuing to strive to be someone better in life" (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:30:03; translated)

## 2. Capabilities and Constraints

- Capability: persistence and slow mobility gain. She walked to the pantry for
  a year, then bought a small car.
  - "At first, I came on foot and then I started coming in a small car... Before, I didn't have a vehicle, until after a year I managed to buy a small car." (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:25:38 and 15:25:55; translated)
- Capability: uses the pantry's rent assistance and knows how to ask.
  - "on two occasions they helped me pay the rent for my home" (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:26:16; translated)
- Constraint: English. It is a source of sadness and a barrier to trying
  anything new.
  - "I get sad or feel a bit down because I don't know much English." (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:28:02; translated)
- Constraint: the pantry's app runs out of food.
  - "when I enter the application, there's almost nothing, sometimes it seems like people don't give space for another person to take." (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:30:28; translated)
- Constraint: reluctance to try unfamiliar programs.
  - "I'm not one to like experimenting with new projects." (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:28:46; translated)

## 3. Meanings and Practices

- Practice of mobility: a year on foot, then a small car. Buying the car was a
  year-long project, and it changed her relationship to the pantry.
- Meaning: mobility is hard-won progress. The car is evidence of "striving to
  be someone better," not a given.
- Practice: staying connected between visits by WhatsApp and text.

## 4. Misalignment

- Tension: translation is not enough. She wants Spanish-speaking staff she can
  build a relationship with, because that is what makes the service feel
  trustworthy.
  - Value at stake: relational trust, and equitable access.
  - "that they speak a little Spanish, not 100% English, but at least some Spanish, which makes you feel reliable, that at least the person understands what you are saying." (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:29:04; translated)
- Tension: the app-based food reservation runs out, and it reads to her as
  people taking more than their share, i.e. a scarcity and fairness problem
  baked into the digital booking model.
  - Value at stake: equitable access, and flexibility for variable need.
  - "when I enter the application, there's almost nothing" (Participant 15 SP.docx, Interviewee 15:30:28; translated)

## 5. Provocation

- Speculative prompt present: no.
- Adjacent, automation reaction (positive): "good, calm, and the best of all,

## Cross-bucket notes

- The "translator is not enough, I want Spanish-speaking staff" point is now
  in four Spanish-language SEC transcripts (03, 04, 11, 15). This is a solid
  synthesis finding: for Spanish-speaking clients the language issue is a
  relational-trust issue, not a transaction-translation issue.
- The app running out of food is a second-order effect of moving reservation
  online: it creates a race, and losers get "almost nothing." Pair with the
  card-shuffler lottery (Ministries of Love) and the twice-a-month cap (Old
  Bethel) as rationing mechanisms clients experience as unfair.
- "A year on foot, then a small car" is a clean image for the capability
  argument: the client closed her own mobility gap through sustained effort,
  with no help from the system.
